Ducks in soup? They serve no master, they got 3 passports: air, land, broth. A pidgeon on a wire hears all trials, but only speeks in crumbs. If u cage a crow, u cage urself, cus in burd law, bars work both ways. 🪶 An remember the Wren Act of ’74 (wich I invented, but its still real in my head) says: “Posseshun is 9/10ths eggscept when its migratory season.” That’s when the court moves, like the birds. How My Sister Became a Bird (and Why Society Should Care)

January 2,2025 by Dennis Reynolds

It has come to my attention — and frankly, it’s been painfully obvious to anyone with functioning eyes — that my sister, Dee Reynolds, is a bird. I don’t mean that in some poetic, whimsical way. I mean she is, functionally, behaviorally, aesthetically… a bird. A tall, awkward, flightless bird, the kind you see pacing nervously around a dumpster. For years, I’ve endured the squawking. The jerky head movements. The beady, darting eyes that suggest a deep evolutionary instinct to flee predators, which, in her case, is any man who shows even the slightest romantic interest. She has the hollow bones — metaphorically, of course, though I wouldn’t be surprised — and the brittle, birdlike frame that could be snapped by a strong breeze or a mildly critical Yelp review. What’s more infuriating is her complete denial. “I’m not a bird,” she says, as if sheer force of will can rewrite millions of years of avian evolution. The public needs to understand the danger here. We are living in a society where bird-people walk among us unchecked. They could be plotting. They could be nesting. My sister could, at any moment, lay an egg, and who would be held responsible? Me. Always me. I will not stand idly by while this… feathered charade continues. Dee, if you’re reading this (which I know you are, because you can’t resist obsessively googling yourself), understand this: your cover is blown. You are a bird. You will always be a bird. And when society finally wakes up to the avian infiltration, don’t expect me to save you. I’ll be too busy living my perfectly human life — in human skin, with human hands, doing human things.
